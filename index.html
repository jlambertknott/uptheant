Norfolk Broads · Wroxham to Stalham

Up the Ant

Down the Bure from Wroxham, past the ruins of St Benet's, then left up the River Ant: through How Hill, across Barton Broad, and on to Neatishead and Stalham. Tap any stop for the history, the wildlife and something to win an argument with.

Windy-day note. The Ant is narrow and winding, so it's fairly sheltered, but Barton Broad is open water and gusts hard across it. Ludham Bridge and Wroxham Bridge are low: check the clearance boards, and remember wind pushes a slow boat sideways under bridges. If the plan changes, the stops below still work as a Bure-only trip.

Talk like a Broadsman

Words you'll see on signs and hear at the staithe.

Staithe
A landing place or quay. Every village has one.
Dyke
A side channel off the river, often leading to a staithe.
Quant
The long pole wherrymen used to push a boat along when the wind died.
Carr
Wet woodland of alder and willow that grows up on old fen.
Rond
The strip of land between the river's edge and the flood bank.
Wherry
The black-sailed Norfolk cargo boat, built to sail close to the wind with one big gaff sail.
Mardle
A good long chat. Also a pond.
Bishy barnabee
A ladybird.
On the huh
Lopsided, askew. See also: someone's mooring.
Squit
Nonsense. As in "talking squit".
